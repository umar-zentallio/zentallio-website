Nexus · Supply Chain

Beauty stock has an expiry date, which turns replenishment into a race rather than a level. The question is never only how much, it is how much and how long has it got.

Cover is read against shelf life, not against a reorder point. Ninety-one days of stock is healthy or fatal depending entirely on what the batch has left.

Batch is the unit, not the SKU. Two deliveries of the same line are two different clocks, and treating them as one is how a write-off happens quietly.

Moving beats marking down. Iris prices a transfer against a discount, so the cheap answer is chosen before the expensive one becomes the only one.

Tester & sampling consumption

Testers are the only stock in retail that is bought to be given away, and they are almost never counted. In a category running 57% gross margin, an untracked tester line is margin leaving the counter one pump at a time.

01

Testers are held as inventory with their own consumption rate, not written off at delivery and forgotten.

02

Consumption per counter is compared against sales of the same line — a tester burning fast next to a shade that is not selling is a signal, not a coincidence.

03

Hygiene replacement runs on a rule with a date, because a tester past its life is a compliance issue before it is a cost one.

04

Sampling is attributed to the sale it produced where the customer is known, which turns a marketing cost into a measurable one.

05

Tester stock carries the same batch and expiry discipline as sale stock, since it comes from the same production run.

04 / 04

Ingredient & recall traceability

Cosmetics is a regulated category applied directly to skin. A recall is not a stock exercise — it is a question about which batch reached which customer, and it has to be answerable in hours.

01

Ingredient and allergen data is held against the batch, so a regulatory change can be assessed across the range without opening a single file.

02

Because the till captures the batch, an affected sale is identifiable — a recall becomes a contactable list rather than a public notice.

03

A flagged batch is blocked at the till by rule across all sixty-three counters at once, not by an email asking staff to check.

04

Market-specific ingredient restrictions are enforced per country, which matters across a European estate.

05

The audit trail is a by-product of trading, not a document assembled after an incident.

Replenishment cycle prediction

Beauty is the rare category where the repeat purchase is close to certain and only the timing is in question. A bottle empties on a schedule; the retailer who knows that schedule owns the refill.

01

Days to run-out is modelled per customer per line at 91.3% accuracy, from size, purchase history and how she actually uses it — not from an average cycle.

02

The prompt lands a week before the bottle is empty. A month after, and she has already replaced it somewhere else.

03

Refill capture at 44% is the number this model exists to move, and it is reported next to repeat purchase rather than hidden in CRM.

04

Predicted refills feed counter replenishment as base demand, so the shade she is coming back for is on the wall when she arrives.

05

The same cycle view is what makes 91.8% replenishment accuracy possible — known demand is easier to stock than guessed demand.
