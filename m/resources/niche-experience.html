Iris · L1 01 / 03

Offline Mode

What the staff member experiences when there is no signal: nothing. The till behaves identically, the guest sees no difference, and no one has to decide what to do about it.

01

Orders, payments and tickets are taken exactly as they would be online — there is no separate offline workflow to remember.

02

Card payments are captured for deferred authorisation within configured risk limits, so a lost connection is not a lost sale.

03

Menus, prices, ticket lists and guest records are cached locally, so an entire service can run from the device.

04

Ticket validation works offline against the cached seating list, which is exactly when a festival gate needs it most.

05

The connection state is visible to a manager but never to a guest, and the till never asks staff to make a judgement call.

Iris · L1 02 / 03

Offline-First Trading & Sync

The architecture underneath — how several devices that were all offline at once converge into one correct ledger, without duplicates, lost orders or a manual reconciliation.

01

The system is offline-first by design rather than online-with-a-fallback, which is the difference between graceful and fragile.

02

Every transaction carries an idempotent identity, so a retry on reconnection can never post the same sale twice.

03

Conflicts — two devices selling the last ticket, stock depleted twice — resolve by defined precedence rather than by whichever synced first.

04

Sync is incremental and resumable, so a device on a weak signal converges rather than repeatedly failing a full upload.

05

Anything that could not resolve automatically is queued for review with its context attached, rather than silently dropped.
