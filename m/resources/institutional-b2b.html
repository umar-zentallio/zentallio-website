05

Iris · L2 02 / 12

No-Show & Headcount Adjuster AI

The same-day correction. Booked is not served — and the gap between them, applied late enough to be accurate and early enough to matter, is where over-production is prevented.

01

Expected no-show is predicted per site and per service, so the production number is adjusted rather than the booking taken at face value.

02

Same-day signals — badge-ins, weather, calendar events, ward occupancy — revise the number while the kitchen can still act on it.

03

The adjustment is applied at a cut-off tuned per site, late enough to be accurate and early enough to change the cook.

04

Systematic over-booking by a department is surfaced as a pattern, which is a conversation with the client rather than a kitchen problem.

05

Actual versus booked is retained per service, which is the evidence behind any billing discussion about headcount.

Headcount Forecasting & Waste Reduction

02

Batch cooking is staged through service, so the last hour is not served from food cooked for the first.

03

Plate waste and counter waste are recorded separately, because over-production and over-portioning need different fixes.

04

Waste by dish and service flows back into the forecast, correcting a repeatedly over-produced dish rather than repeating it.

05

Waste is costed and shown against the contract's food-cost model, which is what makes it a commercial conversation.

The billable headcount is derived exactly as the contract defines it — served, booked, eligible or attended — not as whichever number is easiest to produce.

02

Minimum guarantees and volume bands are applied automatically, including the shortfall charge when a site under-delivers.

03

Every billed figure is traceable to the transactions behind it, which turns a client dispute into a lookup.

04

An independent signal — badge or access data — corroborates the till count where the contract requires it.

05

Variance between forecast, served and billed headcount is monitored, since a persistent gap is either a pricing problem or a data one.
