Most kitchen display systems are just a screen. They take a printed ticket, drop it in a box on a monitor, and call that digital transformation.

The order still routes on habit. The fryer still runs on a lookup table from the menu. And the first anyone hears about a bottleneck is a customer complaint. The screen changed; the kitchen didn't.

Zentallio's KDS starts from a different premise: every ticket, every scan, every station's pace is signal — and a kitchen that reads its own signal can run itself better while the shift is still happening. Iris does the reading. The board is just where that intelligence surfaces, in time to act on it.

The board

One board, every channel

The Expo board is the first thing a shift lead sees, and it's deliberately unified. Every ticket carries its full context — allergy flags, modifiers, rider ETA — so a decision that used to mean walking to three screens now takes one glance.

The intelligence

Where the intelligence actually lives

Underneath the screens, three layers do the work — deterministic where it must be, predictive where it pays, and conversational where a human needs an answer, not a dashboard.

The menu says loaded fries take four and a half minutes. Your fryers, your crew, and tonight's ticket mix make them in seven. Every ready-time on every ticket should say seven — not the number on a laminated card. That middle layer is what changes how a kitchen runs day to day

When one station starts trending slower than its own baseline, the system says so before it becomes a missed promise time — not in the report the morning after.

Resilience

Built to keep running

Offline-ready

A kitchen doesn't get to pause because the internet did. When the cloud connection drops, the KDS keeps taking orders from the POS and floor tablets over the local network, keeps every station in sync, and arms printers as an automatic fallback — nothing waits on a screen that isn't there. Everything queues locally and reconciles the moment the connection returns. A small line on a spec sheet; a very large deal at 7 p.m. on a Friday.

Judgment stays yours

Built for the kitchen it's actually in

None of this replaces judgment — a shift lead can always override a routed ticket, adjust a threshold, or decide Iris should speak up more or less. What it removes is the guesswork: the manual tally at close, the fryer nobody was watching, the ticket that quietly aged past its promise time while everyone was heads-down on the one in front of them.

The kitchen already knows how it's doing. Zentallio's KDS just makes sure someone's listening.
